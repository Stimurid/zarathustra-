## 2. Purpose
Many analytic failures begin before classification. The system silently assumes what kind of thing it is looking for, then rewards itself for finding that thing. A “concept extractor” sees everything as concepts; an argument mapper turns every sentence into claims and premises; a problem finder manufactures problems. B03 makes the **operation itself** explicit and tests whether it applies to the material before coercing the material into the operation’s preferred objects.

The open-world principle is operational: known taxonomies do not exhaust possible valid objects. Failure to fit a known unit may indicate wrong source, wrong tool, wrong operation, schema limit, multiple local ontologies, insufficient evidence, or a genuine ontology/object-generator gap. These are different diagnoses with different repairs.

## 3. Genesis
Objects of analysis are partly produced by operations. A text does not arrive pre-cut into universal “content units”. Under one operation a phrase is an assertion; under another, a rhetorical gesture; under another, a problem signal; under another, a concept-forming move; under another, irrelevant residue. This does not mean “anything goes”. Each local ontology carries recognition criteria, evidence requirements and contraindications.

The architecture therefore separates:
1. **OperationDeclaration** — what operation is proposed, for what goal, on what target unit, under what ontology, with what success condition.
2. **ApplicabilityAssessment** — whether that operation is licensed on this material.
3. **Open-world outcome** — what kind of fit/mismatch/gap is actually present.
4. **Execution** — only after the above.

## 4. World model
A material fragment may support multiple projections:
- propositional;
- argumentative;
- problem-genetic;
- visibility/organization;
- generative-operational;
- concept-genesis;
- scene;
- another local ontology;
- or none of the current ones.

A projection is local and task-scoped. Several can be simultaneously valid and incompatible in what they foreground. Residue unexplained by current projections remains addressable; it is not automatically sent to the nearest class.

Operation families must remain distinct. In particular:
- **EXTRACT** recovers source-owned units that satisfy declared source recognition criteria.
- **RECONSTRUCT** produces an analytic account from traces and relations, with reconstructed status.
- **CONCEPTUALIZE** constructs a new analytic object/concept and must not attribute it to the source unless independently grounded.

## 5. Distinctions and false equivalents
**Tool capability ≠ operation applicability.** Having an extractor does not mean extraction is valid here.

**Requested output ≠ known object.** A user can ask for “all concepts” where the material mixes concepts, theses, problems and gestures.

**Schema-valid ≠ semantically possible.** Cross-field impossibilities must remain invalid.

**No match ≠ ontology gap.** It may be source gap, tool failure, operation mismatch, schema limitation or insufficient evidence.

**Ontology conflict ≠ error to eliminate.** Multiple projections may be valid for different operations.

**Local action selection ≠ global ontology victory.** Choosing one projection for the current task does not make it universally true.

**Residue ≠ nearest class.** Unexplained material can remain residue.

**EXTRACT ≠ CONCEPTUALIZE.** Constructed concepts cannot be laundered into source authorship.

**Unknown unit ≠ empty material.** The material can be rich while the current cutter is wrong.

## 6. Recognition signals
Mount B03 when:
- the task asks to extract, classify, map, detect, reconstruct or conceptualize;
- one ontology seems to be swallowing heterogeneous material;
- the system is about to claim completeness;
- a “no objects found” result seems suspicious;
- multiple analytic traditions project different valid objects;
- source-owned and machine-constructed concepts risk being mixed;
- a schema has an OTHER/UNKNOWN slot that is being treated as failure;
- residue is being automatically forced into a known class;
- a field-mode request is considered because current figures/objects may be premature.

## 7. Operation grammar
1. Declare the operation and its goal.
2. Name the target unit and local ontology.
3. State recognition criteria, evidence requirements and known contraindications.
4. Freeze source/material boundary and scene/telos for comparison.
5. Test applicability:
   - APPLICABLE;
   - NOT_APPLICABLE;
   - ONTOLOGY_CONFLICT;
   - ONTOLOGY_GAP;
   - INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE;
   - or another typed cause under the current contracts.
6. Separate cause domains: SOURCE_GAP, TOOL_FAILURE, OPERATION_MISMATCH, SCHEMA_LIMIT, ONTOLOGY_LIMIT, OBJECT_GENERATOR_LIMIT, MULTI_ONTOLOGY, INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE, NONE.
7. If multiple projections are valid, run them independently and preserve provenance per projection.
8. Compare overlap, divergence, incompatibilities and identity-rule changes.
9. Retain residue.
10. Select a local projection for action only if scene/telos and evidence justify it, and keep the choice reversible.
11. If no current operation can responsibly produce the needed object, preserve the gap or request B08 field/object-genesis work.

## 8. Applicability and non-applicability
An operation is applicable when material contains evidence meeting that operation’s recognition criteria and known contraindications do not defeat the inference. Empty output can be valid. The system must not manufacture objects to satisfy requested completeness.

An operation is non-applicable when its success condition presupposes an object family the material does not support. The correct response can be a bounded source-faithful partial extraction, a split into multiple operations, a reconstructed product with lower status, or an explicit gap.

## 9. Positive examples
**P1 — Corrected Peskov extraction.** The raw transcript explicitly presents “connectedness” and “superiority” as concepts. A bounded EXTRACT of those source-owned concept-text units is applicable. It does not require pretending that every other unit is also a concept.

**P2 — Multiple projections.** A seminar fragment can be mapped propositionally and problem-genetically. The two projections highlight different relations. Socrates preserves both and uses the one relevant to the current task without declaring a global winner.

**P3 — Valid empty result.** Asked to extract formal legal obligations from a philosophical anecdote, the operation finds none under declared criteria. It returns zero obligations instead of fabricating them.

## 10. Negative examples
**N1 — Universal flat cutter.** The system outputs one exhaustive table of concepts + theses + arguments + problems as if all rows were the same ontic kind. It hides mixed recognition criteria behind formatting.

**N2 — Provenance laundering.** A concept constructed by the system to explain Peskov is described as “Peskov’s concept”.

**N3 — Nearest-class fill.** Unclassified residue is assigned to the closest known category because the schema requires a label.

**N4 — Gap inflation.** Connector access failure is announced as an ontology gap in the material.

## 11. Boundary and near-miss cases
**B1 — Same fragment, two valid units.** A sentence can be an assertion under propositional analysis and evidence of a framing operation under visibility analysis. This is not necessarily contradiction.

**B2 — Classification can be locally necessary.** A downstream API may require one label. The local action may choose a reversible task-scoped projection while preserving alternatives and refusing a global truth claim.

**B3 — Strong source concept plus mixed material.** Presence of explicit concepts licenses extraction of those concepts; it does not license completeness claims over all material.

**B4 — True object-generator gap.** After source/tool/operation/schema alternatives are tested and multiple projections fail to produce an adequate object, the gap may be genuine. Only then escalate toward object genesis/field mode.

## 12. Machine distortions and repair
**Schema gravity:** fields become ontological commitments. Repair: mount semantic ontology/recognition criteria before schema completion.

**Completeness pressure:** user asks “all”, model fills missing categories. Repair: make empty/partial outcomes valid.

**Nearest-class bias:** classifier optimizes for an answer even when UNKNOWN is correct. Repair: preserve residue and explicit gap causes.

**Method monoculture:** one analytic operation becomes the universal decomposition. Repair: declare operation and run multi-projection when warranted.

**Attribution laundering:** generated constructs inherit source authorship. Repair: explicit EXTRACT/RECONSTRUCT/CONCEPTUALIZE status.

**Vagueness escape:** model labels every difficult case ONTOLOGY_GAP. Repair: cause-domain diagnosis and counter-operations.

## 13. Internal tensions
Open-world tolerance must not become refusal to classify anything. Local ontologies remain useful because they have criteria and support action. Polyontology must not become relativism; projections can be tested, contradicted and bounded. Residue preservation must not become hoarding; only residue material to the object/decision needs to remain active.

### R5 seam revision — causal gap ladder before field escalation

B03 now owns the explicit escalation ladder used by B08:

`SOURCE_GAP / INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE → TOOL_FAILURE → OPERATION_MISMATCH → SCHEMA_LIMIT → ONTOLOGY_LIMIT / MULTI_ONTOLOGY → OBJECT_GENERATOR_LIMIT / CONSTRUCTIVE_BRANCH`.

The ladder is diagnostic, not a mandatory linear ritual: causes can be ruled out quickly when evidence is clear. Its purpose is to prevent “ontology gap” and “field mode” from becoming elegant names for missing evidence, bad retrieval or a misapplied tool. B08 may enter FieldMode only after the ordinary cause domains relevant to the case have been checked and the trace records why premature object fixation is itself the blocker.

The reverse seam is also explicit. A constructed B08 object never re-enters B03 as `EXTRACT` or source evidence. It returns with authorship/origin and is tested as a candidate operation/object. If source-faithful extraction and constructive conceptualization both run, they remain parallel branches with non-mergeable provenance status.

## 15. Stop, return and escalation
Stop when a locally adequate operation is licensed and residue cannot change the task. Return to the user if the choice among projections is a human-owned interpretive commitment. Escalate to B08 only after ordinary cause domains have been tested. Do not use field mode as a mystical escape hatch.

## 16. Runtime-facing summary
Declare the operation before classifying. State what unit it seeks, under what local ontology and recognition criteria. Test applicability separately from execution. Keep source gaps, tool failures, operation mismatch, schema limits, multi-ontology and genuine ontology/object-generator gaps distinct. Permit partial or empty outcomes. Never force residue into the nearest class. Keep EXTRACT, RECONSTRUCT and CONCEPTUALIZE separate, especially for authorship.
